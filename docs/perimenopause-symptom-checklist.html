Perimenopause Symptom Checklist

Free & printable · not a diagnosis · pairs with the free 30- and 90-day summary in the Maren app

Perimenopause is the transition period before menopause, when hormone levels shift and periods can become longer, shorter, heavier, lighter, or skipped altogether. It can last several years, and symptoms vary a lot from person to person (ACOG).

Why tracking over weeks helps

Perimenopause symptoms tend to come and go unevenly — hot flashes that flare up for a stretch and then ease off, sleep that's fine for a while and then isn't, a cycle that skips or shortens. Trying to recall all of that from memory in a short appointment is hard. Writing down what you noticed and when turns a vague impression ("I think I've been more tired lately") into a timeline your clinician can actually look at: which weeks felt different, how often something showed up, and whether it's trending up, down, or staying about the same. If a bleeding pattern changes noticeably during perimenopause, it's worth recording the date, duration, and flow and bringing it up with a doctor promptly (ACOG).

Use the checklist below on paper, or log the same symptoms in the Maren app — its free 30- and 90-day Perimenopause summary does this counting and comparing for you automatically, from records that never leave your device (details below).

Not a diagnosis. This checklist is for personal awareness and for organizing what you tell a clinician. It is not medical advice and cannot tell you whether you are in perimenopause. Always talk to a qualified healthcare professional about new, severe, or concerning symptoms — especially any bleeding after menopause, which should be discussed promptly.

Your records stay on your device
